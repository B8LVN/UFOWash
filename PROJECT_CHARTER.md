# UFO WASH — Project Charter

## 1. Project identity

**Working title:** UFO WASH  
**Genre:** First-person 3D simulation / job simulator / business management / incremental progression / automation.  
**Initial platform:** Windows PC.  
**Potential later platforms:** Steam distribution; Android only after the PC experience is validated.  
**Engine baseline:** Unity 6.6 (6000.6), URP, C#.  
**Mode:** Single-player first. Multiplayer is explicitly out of initial scope.

## 2. One-sentence vision

Start as the only worker in a tiny alien spacecraft cleaning station, personally clean every arriving UFO, reinvest the earnings into better tools and facilities, hire staff and automate the workflow, then expand into a profitable intergalactic servicing business.

## 3. Non-negotiable identity

- **UFO WASH remains the game.** Do not replace it with a scrapyard, factory, farming or unrelated simulator.
- Keep the alien spacecraft cleaning and servicing business at the centre of every feature.
- The game is a first-person, physically interactive 3D simulation—not a menu-driven idle game.
- Manual work must be satisfying and useful, especially early in progression.
- Automation must become a major mid/late-game reward.
- Money must unlock new capabilities, spaces, customers, equipment and business scale—not just larger numbers.
- The player must see the station and operation physically improve.
- Quality and cohesion matter more than feature count.

## 4. Design references

Use Moon Sweepers as a reference for the satisfying “start with almost nothing → do tangible work → collect/ship/sell → upgrade tools/capacity → deploy vehicles/depots → automate” progression.

Use Schedule I as a reference for “personally run the operation → earn → acquire property/equipment → hire employees → manage/automate a growing business.”

Do not copy exact mechanics, UI, level layouts, models, characters, names, branding, sound, or visual assets. Extract abstract design principles and create original UFO WASH solutions.

## 5. Player promise

The player should repeatedly feel:

1. “I can do this job myself, and my actions visibly matter.”
2. “I earned enough to buy something that changes how I work.”
3. “My station is visibly more capable than it was before.”
4. “I can delegate or automate a bottleneck that used to consume my time.”
5. “I now have a bigger opportunity to work toward.”

## 6. What success looks like

A new player can understand the first job without a long tutorial, enjoy operating the tools, see the UFO become visibly cleaner, receive a clear payment/reward, make a meaningful purchase, and understand how that purchase improves the next job. Later, the same player can build and observe an automated workflow and still has interesting management choices.

## 7. Roles

**Game Director / Product Owner (user):** creative direction, prioritization approval, playtesting, feedback and approval of major changes/costs.

**Lead Developer / AI development partner:** audit, research, technical design, implementation, debugging, asset/workflow recommendations, documentation and quality gates. The agent must be honest about its inability to run the user's local Unity Editor unless it has actual tool access.

## 8. Financial and scope discipline

- No paid plugins, software, services or assets without explicit approval.
- Prefer a small, polished vertical slice over a broad unfinished game.
- Do not commit to multiplayer, multiple planets, huge open worlds or complex production chains before the core loop is proven.
- Do not estimate commercial revenue as guaranteed.
- Any major scope increase requires a written decision-log entry and an explanation of cost, risk and expected player value.
