# UFO WASH — Game Design Document (GDD)

**Version:** 0.1 · **Status:** Pre-production baseline

## High concept
A first-person sci-fi cleaning and business simulation. Customers arrive in alien spacecraft that are dirty, unusual or contaminated. The player operates cleaning equipment, restores vehicles, earns credits, improves the facility and gradually builds an extraterrestrial service business.

## Player fantasy
Start with a modest wash bay and personally do satisfying work. Turn grime into clean, polished spacecraft; earn credits; upgrade tools and infrastructure; eventually employ alien workers and automate a thriving operation.

## Design pillars
1. **Hands-on satisfying cleaning:** visible dirt removal, responsive spray, foam, water, surface feedback and sound.
2. **Physical place and presence:** movement and equipment interactions happen in a tangible facility.
3. **Readable progression:** upgrades produce noticeable improvements or new capabilities.
4. **Business growth:** move from owner-operator to management and automation.
5. **Stylized sci-fi identity:** memorable UFO silhouettes, readable shapes, bright effects and practical facility design.

## Core loop
UFO arrives → inspect job/vehicle → clean using tools → meet quality threshold → complete service → receive credits → purchase upgrades/expand → serve new or more demanding customers.

## MVP experience
One compact wash bay; first-person movement and camera; one UFO; one spray tool; measurable dirt state and visible feedback; job completion and credits; one upgrade; simple interaction UI.

## Longer-term (not MVP)
Multiple vehicle classes and grime types; equipment tiers; construction and additional bays; customer queue; employees and assignments; automation; progression unlocks; additional locations; challenges/events.

## Perspective
First-person is the MVP direction, subject to prototype validation. It prioritizes immersion and direct tool interaction.

## Setting and tone
A colorful, playful alien industrial world. The facility feels practical, lived-in and upgradeable. Humor and strange spacecraft can carry personality.

## UX principles
- Clear contextual interaction prompts.
- Readable job status and cleaning progress.
- Remappable controls and device awareness.
- Minimize menu friction; keep player in the physical space.

## Release direction
No monetization design is locked during MVP. PC release viability first; Steam is a potential distribution target. Android is a later adaptation, not an MVP constraint.

## Open design questions
Art style and fidelity; cleaning tool variety and tactile feel; vehicle/customer personality; management depth vs. direct action balance; upgrade pacing.
